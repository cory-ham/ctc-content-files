A $52M Activewear + Supplement Brand

Modeled CM$ grew 256% on 93% tracked-store revenue growth — Q3 '25 vs Q3 '26
Expand ↓
Where the business started
~$52M in tracked store revenue in 2025. Established marketing team, but wanted more consistent online profitability and less dependence on major sale periods.
Two discoveries

1. Advertising targets could pass while acquisition economics missed

Found: Channels could beat ROAS targets while new-customer revenue fell short.

Did: Compared six Meta attribution windows against aMER and recommended a 3.4x seven-day-click ROAS floor.

PS: Channel efficiency targets

2. Spending around promotions could erode contribution

Found: After a promotion, revenue remained above plan but spend outpaced it and acquisition efficiency missed target.

Did: Pulled back low-return Meta spend. Called for further Google non-brand and TikTok reductions after the promotion.

PS: Daily spending plans
Why a founder should care

A 2027 plan can give the existing team shared forecasts, inventory-ready priorities and spending guardrails.

02

A $17M Furniture Brand

DTC revenue grew 56%, modeled CM grew 119% — Q3 '25 vs Q3 '26
Expand ↓
Where the business started
After rapid growth, founders reported sales down 20% in 2025. Wanted an aggressive recovery, but initial forecast fell below their ambitions and they had limited incrementality evidence.
Two discoveries

1. Blended reporting hid different businesses

Found: Combining trade, showroom and online sales obscured the economics paid media could manage.

Did: Agreed to separate views in February. By April 16, distinct DTC and B2B reporting instances confirmed.

PS: Revenue forecasting

2. Reduce dependence on one hero product

Found: Long lead times and stockouts constrained the hero product while alternatives showed traction.

Did: Reduced hero-product spend, invested in available alternatives, corrected an out-of-stock landing page. August review confirmed 21% more July orders.

PS: Financial planning and spend allocation
Why a founder should care

Plan around what can ship, not just what sold before. A 2027 plan can give the existing team shared forecasts, inventory-ready priorities and spending guardrails.

03

A $22M Home Textiles Brand

Revenue grew 26%, modeled CM grew 21% — Q3 '25 vs Q3 '26
Expand ↓
Where the business started
Produced five ads daily, yet struggled with new-customer acquisition and neglected its evergreen catalog. Marketing lead wanted a dependable acquisition base beyond seasonal peaks.
Two discoveries

1. Separate everyday acquisition from launch spikes

Found: A sprawling catalog and seasonal spikes did not establish a dependable evergreen acquisition base.

Did: After the seasonal campaign ended, copied winning ads and increased evergreen investment.

PS: Marketing calendar and channel planning

2. More segmentation was not automatically better

Found: Non-brand shopping struggled even after narrowing the assortment.

Did: Proposed halving campaign count on Sept 1. Sept 8 review confirmed consolidation improved spend, revenue and ROAS.

PS: Channel planning and spend targets
Why a founder should care

A bigger catalog and more ads don’t guarantee better margins. A 2027 plan can give the existing team evergreen priorities, a launch calendar and channel tests with margin guardrails.

04

A $20M Apparel Brand

eCommerce revenue grew 67% ($2.46M → $4.10M) — Q3 '25 vs Q3 '26
Expand ↓
Where the business started
Needed to rebuild new-customer acquisition after missing early-2026 plan. Creator output was abundant, but spending was concentrated in a minority of ads and many assets received too little exposure to evaluate.
Two discoveries

1. Use customer value to choose creative investments

Found: 61 creator ads took 82% of creator-ad spend; 712 spent under $200.

Did: July’s ad plan used inventory as a base score and repeat-purchase value as a multiplier. By August, an ads calculator guided creator and style selection.

PS: Creative demand and financial planning

2. Distinguish underexposure from creative failure

Found: High-conviction creative could receive too little spend to produce useful evidence.

Did: Products spending under $100 in seven days were routed into an inventory-checked test. Successful campaigns earned more budget at target efficiency.

PS: Daily spending and efficiency targets
Why a founder should care

A 2027 plan can link the existing team’s inventory calendar, creative requirements and testing budgets. Production volume is not enough: the right products need a meaningful test.

05

An 8-Figure Hair-Care Brand

Revenue grew 66%, modeled CM grew 94% — Aug–Sep '25 vs Aug–Sep '26
Expand ↓
Where the business started
Bootstrapped brand wanted growth without sacrificing profitability. Acquisition costs above $50, limited near-term repeat-purchase lift, and materially different regional delivery costs.
Two discoveries

1. Set acquisition boundaries before spending ambitions

Found: Committing to spend and hoping acquisition costs worked out conflicted with the brand’s customer economics.

Did: Replaced a ~$300K monthly run rate with a ~$210K August plan and locked efficiency targets. Campaign migration completed July 31.

PS: Financial forecasts and daily targets

2. The same return target did not mean the same contribution

Found: Cost of delivery was 58.4% of order revenue internationally vs 45.6% in the US.

Did: By Aug 14, acquisition-efficiency targets were split: 1.36x for US, 1.54x internationally, with separate platform targets.

PS: Channel efficiency targets
Why a founder should care

A 2027 plan can give finance and media shared acquisition limits by market, allowing creative testing to earn additional spend.

06

A $7M Footwear Accessories Brand

Net revenue grew 32%, modeled CM grew 30% — Q3 '25 vs Q3 '26
Expand ↓
Where the business started
Wanted more DTC growth, but the team worried weak reported Meta ROAS meant Facebook was struggling. Creative production was constrained at ~30–40 ads monthly, below the evergreen need discussed in planning.
Two discoveries

1. The platform target was not the business target

Found: Click-only ROAS and business acquisition efficiency told different stories.

Did: Retained an acquisition-efficiency spending guardrail, completed an April Meta geo-holdout, incorporated a ~1.33x total-revenue incrementality factor into Statlas.

PS: Incrementality-informed allocation

2. More media was not the only investment

Found: Product-in-hand creator assets repeatedly won, but production limited new options.

Did: Leadership approved moving media budget into creator production in May. By July 2 the new batch was live.

PS: Creative and financial planning
Why a founder should care

A 2027 plan can specify economic guardrails and production capacity, giving the existing team a measured plan to execute and adjust.

07

A $30M Accessories Brand

Revenue grew 25%, modeled CM grew 17% — Q3 '25 vs Q3 '26
Expand ↓
Where the business started
Committed to inventory for higher sales while efficiency cuts left the founder worried about growth and cash flow. Challenge: balancing clearance, returning customers, and a specific contribution requirement.
Two discoveries

1. Choose between liquidation and contribution

Found: Dedicated liquidation campaigns moved aged inventory near break-even, conflicting with September’s contribution requirement.

Did: On Aug 31, confirmed those campaigns were off and drop assets consolidated into evergreen campaigns.

PS: Financial forecasts and allocation

2. Returning revenue needed an operational check

Found: A disabled loyalty flow was credited with ~$60K monthly revenue in June and July.

Did: After an escalation, the team verified and turned the loyalty flow back on.

PS: Returning-customer planning
Why a founder should care

A 2027 plan can give finance, media and lifecycle owners shared contribution requirements, inventory exceptions and retention checks.

08

Omnichannel: Are You Funding Demand or Capture?

New Prophit System add-ons — DTC + TikTok Shop + Amazon, cascade measurement
Expand ↓
The angle
More channels can mean less clarity. A customer discovers you in one place and buys in another. Without understanding that spillover, you end up funding the channel that captures the sale while underfunding the one that creates demand.
Two discoveries

1. Where the sale happens is not where demand starts

Explore how unseen spillover between DTC, Amazon and TikTok Shop can change where a brand should invest.

PS: Cascade measurement

2. Measure the whole business, then understand the relationships

Combine actual revenue and costs across channels first. Media incrementality and brand-specific sales-channel cascade analysis are separate layers that inform allocation.

PS: Total-business forecasting
Prophit System connection
Luke: extending the existing DTC system across Amazon and TikTok Shop. Channel and total-business forecasts, daily P&L targets in Statlas, brand-specific cascade analysis, and a TikTok Shop audit. Connect those capabilities to clearer 2027 inventory, media and resourcing decisions.
Why a founder should care

More places to sell should create more ways to grow, not more ways to misread the business. Understand what creates demand, where it converts, and where the next dollar should go.
